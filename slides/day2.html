01
What changes with a pretrained forecaster

A zero-shot forecaster needs no training on your series, but it is scored like any other method

  • Input: each series' history up to the origin. No product, no depot, no features unless you pass covariates.
  • Output: quantiles for the next 28 days.
  • No fit on Bustan's data, so no tuning budget, and nothing to retrain each month.
  • Same windows as the benchmarks, or the comparison means nothing.

Chronos-2

A pretrained model for univariate, multivariate and covariate-informed forecasting, trained on synthetic data. Weights under Apache-2.0.

Chronos-2's paper describes zero-shot forecasting with covariates and in-context learning across a group of series; its pretraining uses synthetic datasets. TimesFM 2.5 is the other example in the tool slot; its weights on Hugging Face are also under Apache-2.0 (checked November 2026; the catalog's note of non-commercial weights for TimesFM did not match the model page). Sources: Ansari et al., Chronos-2 (arXiv 2510.15821); Hugging Face model pages amazon/chronos-2 and google/timesfm-2.5-200m-pytorch.

Two numbers per series and origin

Divide every error by the series' own scale, or the staples decide alone

ScoreWhat it measuresRead
MASEmean absolute error of the median, divided by the in-sample error of last week's copybelow 1 beats the naive in-sample
Scaled quantile losspinball loss over the 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9 quantiles, same scalerewards sharp bands that still hold
80% coverageshare of days inside the 0.1 to 0.9 bandshould be near 80%

The scaled quantile loss here is twice the mean pinball loss over the three quantiles, divided by the MASE scale, so that its median part alone equals MASE. Scaled errors follow Hyndman and Athanasopoulos. A dairy line sells hundreds of cases a day and a specialty import two: unscaled, the dairy line is the whole average. Sources: Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, Forecasting: Principles and Practice, 3rd edition, chapter 5 (otexts.com/fpp3/accuracy.html).

The loss behind every quantile

The pinball loss charges a quantile forecast unequally for being low and being high

def pinball(y, q, tau):
    d = y - q
    return np.maximum(tau * d, (tau - 1) * d)

# tau = 0.9: a forecast 10 below costs 9,
# a forecast 10 above costs 1

Tomorrow's decision

The same asymmetry is the newsvendor's: when a shortage costs nine times a leftover, order the 0.9 quantile. Day 3 starts here.

This is pre-work material; check that it landed. Ask: which quantile minimises the expected pinball loss with tau 0.9? The 0.9 quantile of the demand distribution. That one fact connects today's scores to tomorrow's orders.

Classifying from the series itself

Two numbers describe a demand pattern: how often it sells, and how much the sizes vary

  • ADI: average days between non-zero days over the last year.
  • CV squared: variability of the non-zero sizes.
  • In the lab: short under 180 days of history; intermittent when ADI is 1.32 or more; the rest regular.

A class has a date

A product launched in December is short in October and regular by May. Classify at the origin you forecast from.

The lab's classes match the generator's answer key exactly for this data (the crosstab in Lab 2.2), which is a check of the code, not a property of real data: real catalogues blur the lines. Ramadan items are known from the product master, not inferred: use what the business already knows.

Recap from DS-304, applied

A short series borrows strength from other series, or it has nothing to learn from

Global model

The LightGBM learns from all 400 series at once, so a new product starts with its category's pattern.

Zero-shot, one series

Sees only the product's own few weeks; the stand-in falls back to its last eight weeks.

In context, many series

Models that read a group of related series together can borrow without training.

DS-304 Day 4 Session 2 covered new and short series; this slide is a pointer, not a lesson. Chronos-2's group attention shares information across series in a group: that is a claim to test in the capstone, not to assume. Sources: Chronos-2 (arXiv 2510.15821).

Session 3

Holidays that move

  • Show why a yearly lag must respect both the week and the Hijri year
  • Supply Ramadan and Eid as known future covariates
  • Measure error in holiday weeks separately from ordinary weeks
03
Why holidays break yearly lags

Demand follows the week and the Hijri year at once, and they do not line up

364 days

52 weeks: same weekday, but Ramadan has moved about eleven days.

354 days

One Hijri year: right Ramadan day, wrong weekday.

357 days

51 weeks: same weekday, within three days of the Hijri date.

Ramadan 1446 began on 1 March 2025; Ramadan 1447 on 18 February 2026 (Umm al-Qura).

Fridays sell about a third of a weekday at Bustan's depots, so a lag that lands on the wrong weekday adds a large error every week. DS-304 used Hijri regressors in a regression; today the same knowledge is passed to a pretrained model as covariates. The dates are those used by the generator (labs/prepare_data.py).

Passing the calendar

The covariates for the past and for the next 28 days go in the same call

pipe = Chronos2Pipeline.from_pretrained("amazon/chronos-2")

Session 4

Ensembles worth their cost

  • Build a stacking set from rolling-origin forecasts without leakage
  • Fit non-negative stacking weights and test them on later windows
  • Report the improvement over the best single model with a bootstrap interval
04
Recap from DS-304, then one step further

The simple average is hard to beat; learned weights must earn their extra risk

Simple average

No weights to estimate, so nothing to overfit. Works when the members are comparably good.

Stacking

Weights learned from earlier forecasts. Wins when the members differ in quality, if the weights are estimated honestly.

Forecasting: Principles and Practice notes that simple combinations are often hard to beat. DS-304 Day 4 Session 3 showed it on Shams Power's feeders. Here the members are very unequal (the floor and the tuned LightGBM), which is exactly when the simple average suffers. Sources: Hyndman and Athanasopoulos, FPP3 chapter 13 (otexts.com/fpp3/combinations.html).

How the weights are learned

Weights for an origin come only from windows that ended before it

Scored originWeights fitted on
2026-01-042025-10-05, 2025-11-16
2026-02-15the three before it
2026-03-15the four before it
2026-05-10the five before it
The rolling-origin forecasts are the out-of-fold predictions of time series. Each was made without its own answers.

The same idea on tables is out-of-fold stacking: the meta-model learns from predictions each base model made on rows it did not train on. On series, folds are origins and only earlier ones may train the weights. The best single model is also chosen on earlier windows; otherwise it gets an advantage the stack does not.
