The holiday season that moves eleven days a year (Day 2) is a fourth, slower shift of the same kind: a pattern the training window holds in different places. Ask the room which of the three a model can be protected from without any northern outcomes. Only covariate shift; the other two need labels.

Recap from DS-305, then what breaks

Split conformal holds 90% of the time only if new orders are exchangeable with the calibration orders

scores = np.abs(y_cal - model.predict(X_cal))   # log hours
k = int(np.ceil((n + 1) * 0.9))
q = np.sort(scores)[k - 1]                      # interval: prediction ± q
Lab 4.1's adversarial AUC of 0.95 says the northern orders are not exchangeable with the old ones.

Split conformal belongs to DS-305, a prerequisite; if participants have not met it, take three minutes on this slide: sort the calibration errors, take the 90th percentile with the small-sample correction, add and subtract it. The intervals are on log hours, so in hours they are multiplicative: from 0.37 to 2.71 times the prediction in the course run.

Correcting for covariate shift

Weight each calibration order by how northern it looks

Classifierp(north | x), out of fold
→
Ratiow(x) = p / (1 - p)
→
Weighted quantileof calibration scores, plus the new order's own weight at infinity
Takeaway. If too few calibration orders look like a new one, its interval is infinite: an honest "we do not know".

Weighted conformal prediction under covariate shift is from Tibshirani, Barber, Candès and Ramdas (2019). It keeps the coverage guarantee when only p(x) changes and the weights are right. Keep the classifier simple and score it out of fold: in-sample probabilities near 0 and 1 make a few weights enormous. Sources: arXiv 1904.06019.

Answer: D. Mention Mondrian (group-conditional) conformal as the tool for per-segment coverage once the segment has its own calibration data.

About a minute on a laptop; quick mode keeps all old orders because fewer make the weights noisy (with a third of them, weighting lowered coverage in a trial run). Watch for weights computed from in-sample probabilities and for a weighted quantile that leaves out the test order's own weight; both make coverage look better than it is.

What adoption requires

A model is adopted when accuracy, licence, data and cost all allow it

Accuracy

Against a tuned baseline, with intervals, under the deployment's shift, by subgroup.

Licence

Weights and outputs allowed for commercial production use.

Data

Classification, personal data, where it may be processed.

Cost

Running and monitoring every cycle, against the gain in riyals.

Any one of the four can block adoption on its own. The lab finds that the licence blocks one candidate outright, the data blocks every option outside the Kingdom for personal data, and cost blocks nothing.

Self-hosting in Riyadh is allowed for every candidate. Google Colab counts as outside the Kingdom: fine for synthetic course data only.

The Personal Data Protection Law, Article 29, allows a transfer only for listed purposes, with an adequate level of protection and the minimum data needed. The Regulation on Personal Data Transfer outside the Kingdom requires a risk assessment before a transfer (Article 7) and, in its exempted cases, safeguards such as standard contractual clauses, binding common rules or a certificate (Article 4). The National Data Governance Policies define the classification levels for public entities; Bustan, a private company, applies the same levels in its own register by choice. Detect
